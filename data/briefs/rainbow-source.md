# How a primary rainbow forms

Source notes based on the US National Weather Service, Flagstaff office,
“How Do Rainbows Form?” https://www.weather.gov/fgz/Rainbow

White sunlight contains a range of colors. A ray changes direction when it crosses from air into a water droplet. Different colors bend by slightly different amounts.

Some of the light reflects off the back inside surface of the droplet. The primary rainbow involves one internal reflection. The light changes direction again when it leaves the water and enters the air.

The emerging colors travel in slightly different directions. Drops at different positions send different colors toward the same observer. Many illuminated droplets together produce the visible bow.

To see a primary rainbow in rain, the observer faces the illuminated droplets with the Sun behind them.

The Optical Society of America tutorial hosted at Florida State University explains that water bends blue light more than red light. https://osa.magnet.fsu.edu/tutorials/whatisarainbow.html
